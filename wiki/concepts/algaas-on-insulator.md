# AlGaAs-on-insulator

> AlGaAs-on-insulator is a high-index-contrast III-V photonic platform that places AlGaAs waveguides or resonators on a low-index insulator, giving compact confinement while preserving AlGaAs's strong chi(2), chi(3), and low telecom nonlinear loss.

## Origin

The platform appears in this batch as a turning point from older GaAs/AlGaAs ridge, Bragg, and superlattice devices toward compact nonlinear integrated circuits: milliwatt-level comb generation — [[frequency-comb-algaas-oi-pu]], octave supercontinuum — [[octave-supercontinuum-algaas-oi-kuyken]], ultrabright entangled photon-pair microrings — [[algaas-oi-entangled-photon-pair-microring-steiner]], and high-throughput comb communications — [[chip-frequency-comb-parallel-data-hu]].

## How it works

AlGaAs provides large nonlinear coefficients and a tunable band gap; the insulator cladding gives strong confinement and dispersion control. The combination is especially useful at telecom wavelengths where AlGaAs can avoid the severe two-photon absorption that limits silicon.

## Trade-offs / Contrasts with

- vs older AlGaAs ridge/BRW devices — better confinement and smaller footprints, but more demanding wafer bonding, substrate removal, and nanofabrication.
- vs [[silicon-nitride]] — much higher nonlinearity and possible active integration; SiN often has lower passive loss and broader foundry maturity.
- vs bulk/periodically poled nonlinear crystals — smaller, chip-compatible, and potentially active, but usually more sensitive to fabrication loss and thermal/process variations.
