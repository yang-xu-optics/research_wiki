# Strong-field quantum optics

> Strong-field quantum optics treats intense light-matter interactions with both the matter and the optical field quantized, asking what happens to photon statistics, squeezing, entanglement, and measurement backaction in regimes usually described by classical laser fields.

## Origin

This direction grows from the tension between two successful approximations: attosecond/HHG physics usually classicalizes the driving field, while quantum optics usually works at low photon number and perturbative nonlinearities. The HHG papers bridge those regimes theoretically and experimentally — [[quantum-optical-nature-hhg-gorlach]], [[evidence-quantum-optical-nature-hhg-theidel]], [[quantum-optics-optical-coherence-hhg-stammer]].

## How it works

The key move is to stop treating the strong laser as a fixed c-number waveform. Instead, the field modes can exchange photons with matter, become entangled with each other, and carry measurable quantum statistics after the interaction. In HHG, this makes the harmonic spectrum only one projection of a richer quantum state.

## Trade-offs / Contrasts with

- vs standard quantum optics — much higher photon numbers and broader spectra, but harder state reconstruction.
- vs standard strong-field physics — retains attosecond electron dynamics, but adds field correlations and measurement theory.
- vs [[integrated-quantum-photonics]] — less chip-scale and more extreme-field today, but offers access to XUV/attosecond quantum states.

## Open questions

- What are the minimal measurements needed to certify nonclassicality in intense HHG fields?
- Can strong-field quantum optics become a source technology for quantum photonics, or mainly a diagnostic of light-matter dynamics?
