# Critical HHG simulation framework

> A concrete theory/simulation plan for a paper on HHG squeezing and entanglement generated by ferroelectric or long-range-correlated matter near a phase transition.

## Core claim

The paper should argue that HHG is not only an ultrafast spectrum of a driven material, but an input-output map from connected matter current correlations to quantum correlations of emitted harmonic modes. Near a phase transition, the matter correlation length and susceptibility can increase, so harmonic squeezing and entanglement can be enhanced, reshaped, or made criticality-dependent.

## Theory backbone

Start from a driven matter Hamiltonian plus quantized emitted modes:

`H(t) = H_m[A_cl(t), lambda] + sum_n hbar omega_n a_n^dag a_n - J[A_cl(t), lambda] A_q(t)`.

Here `A_cl(t)` is the strong classical pump, `lambda` tunes distance to the correlated or ferroelectric transition, `J(t)` is the total current or polarization-current operator, and `A_q(t)` contains weak quantized harmonic modes.

Treat the emitted modes perturbatively but the driven matter dynamics nonperturbatively:

`a_n,out = a_n,in + i g_n int dt f(t) exp(i omega_n t) J(t)`.

Then harmonic photon statistics are determined by connected source correlators:

`<delta a_m^dag delta a_n> ~ g_m g_n int dt dt' exp[-i omega_m t + i omega_n t'] <delta J(t) delta J(t')>`,

`<delta a_m delta a_n> ~ g_m g_n int dt dt' exp[i omega_m t + i omega_n t'] <delta J(t) delta J(t')>`.

This makes the paper modular: any matter model that produces `J(t)` and `<delta J(t) delta J(t')>` can be converted into harmonic spectra, squeezing, and entanglement witnesses.

## Minimal models

### Model A: universal correlated emitter

Use a transverse-field Ising or long-range Ising chain as a controllable long-range-correlated emitter:

`H_m = -J0 sum_<ij> sigma_i^z sigma_j^z - Gamma sum_i sigma_i^x - h(t) sum_i sigma_i^z`.

The strong optical drive enters through `h(t)` or a nonlinear dipole operator. Tune `Gamma / J0` through the quantum critical point. This model is not meant to be a chemically literal ferroelectric. It proves the mechanism: as matter correlations grow, harmonic-mode covariance and entanglement grow.

### Model B: displacive ferroelectric soft-mode chain

Use a quantum phi-four or soft-mode lattice:

`H_m = sum_i [P_i^2/(2M) + r Q_i^2/2 + u Q_i^4/4] + K/2 sum_<ij> (Q_i - Q_j)^2 - E_cl(t) sum_i Z* Q_i`.

Tune `r` through the paraelectric-ferroelectric transition. Define the emitted polarization/current from the driven soft coordinate plus a nonlinear electronic response, for example:

`P_NL(t) = sum_i [alpha_1 Q_i + alpha_3 Q_i^3 + beta Q_i E_cl(t)^2 + ...]`.

This model captures soft-mode enhancement, domain-like correlations, and critical slowing down.

### Model C: HHG-active correlated solid

For the more realistic second half of the paper, couple electrons to a correlated order parameter:

`H_e[A,Q] = -sum_<ij> t_ij(Q) exp[-i A_cl(t) r_ij] c_i^dag c_j + sum_i Delta(Q_i) c_i^dag c_i + H_int`.

The HHG current is `J(t) = -partial H_e / partial A`. The order parameter `Q_i` can be treated quantum mechanically for small systems, semiclassically with Gaussian fluctuations for larger systems, or by MPS/TEBD in 1D.

## Simulation ladder

1. **Null baseline.** Independent emitters or uncorrelated soft modes. Confirm ordinary HHG spectra can appear without strong squeezing/entanglement.
2. **Turn on correlations.** Sweep `Gamma/J0`, `r`, correlation range, or domain coupling. Track how connected current correlators grow.
3. **Convert to photonic observables.** Use the input-output equations to compute harmonic intensities, covariance matrices, squeezing spectra, and entanglement witnesses.
4. **Critical scaling.** Plot nonclassical witnesses versus distance from criticality, correlation length, system size, and dephasing.
5. **Noise audit.** Add thermal fluctuations, dephasing, domain disorder, and classical pump noise. Separate classical excess noise from genuine squeezing or entanglement.
6. **Ferroelectric readout proposal.** Translate dimensionless parameters into qualitative regimes for ferroelectric or quantum-paraelectric candidates.

## Observables

- Harmonic yield: `I_n = <a_n^dag a_n>`.
- Source correlations: `C_J(t,t') = <delta J(t) delta J(t')>`.
- Spectral covariance: `N_mn = <delta a_m^dag delta a_n>`, `M_mn = <delta a_m delta a_n>`.
- Single-mode squeezing: minimum quadrature variance of harmonic `n`.
- Two-mode squeezing: minimum joint quadrature variance for harmonic pair `(m,n)`.
- Entanglement: Gaussian logarithmic negativity or Duan-Simon criterion from the covariance matrix.
- Nonclassical photon statistics: `g2`, antibunching, and Cauchy-Schwarz violation where feasible.

## Main figures

1. Schematic: correlated ferroelectric or long-range emitter driven by a strong pulse, emitting quantum-correlated harmonics.
2. Matter panel: correlation length or connected current correlation versus tuning parameter.
3. HHG panel: harmonic spectrum across the transition.
4. Quantum-optics panel: covariance matrix `|M_mn|` or squeezing matrix among harmonics.
5. Criticality panel: squeezing/log-negativity versus distance from transition, showing an optimum near but not necessarily at criticality.
6. Robustness panel: thermal noise, dephasing, and domain disorder separate classical critical noise from nonclassical witnesses.

## Paper thesis

The strongest version of the paper is not "ferroelectrics produce larger HHG." It is:

> Critical or long-range-correlated matter provides a tunable many-body mediator that maps connected polarization/current correlations into measurable squeezing and entanglement of high-harmonic light.

This framing makes the work broader than one material and connects HHG, quantum optics, and correlated-matter spectroscopy.

The same source-correlation framework also has a perturbative nonlinear-optics limit for SHG, THG, SPDC, and four-wave mixing. That limit is useful as a control problem or companion paper: it asks whether critical matter correlations modify few-mode quantum nonlinear optics before the nonperturbative HHG regime is reached — [[critical-correlations-perturbative-nonlinear-optics]].

## First implementation target

The first numerical implementation should use Model A or Model B with exact diagonalization for small systems. That gives clean many-body correlations and exact two-time current correlators. After validating the input-output covariance pipeline, move to Model C or to MPS/semiclassical fluctuation methods for more realistic solid-state HHG.

## Risks

- A classical critical point can create large noise but no nonclassical squeezing. The paper needs witnesses that cannot be explained by a positive classical stochastic field.
- Exact diagonalization may only access small systems, so finite-size scaling is essential.
- A realistic ferroelectric under strong fields may suffer dephasing, heating, and domain averaging. This should be presented as a target for later experiments, not as the only proof of the theory.
