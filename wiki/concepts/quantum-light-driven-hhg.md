# Quantum-light-driven HHG

> Quantum-light-driven HHG asks how high-harmonic generation changes when the driving field is not a classical coherent pulse, but a nonclassical state such as [[bright-squeezed-vacuum]] or structured squeezed light.

## Origin

The conceptual opening comes from quantum-optical HHG theory, but the batch includes experimental and theoretical moves into BSV-driven HHG, squeezed-light attosecond synthesis, quantum-light symmetry breaking, and classically forbidden geometries — [[high-harmonic-generation-driven-by-quantum-light-gorlach]], [[high-harmonic-generation-bright-squeezed-vacuum-rasputnyi]], [[attosecond-pulse-synthesis-squeezed-light-hhg-wang]], [[structured-squeezed-light-forbidden-hhg-rivera-dean]].

## How it works

The driver no longer has a single deterministic electric field. Its photon-number, quadrature, polarization, and spatial fluctuations become physical inputs to the strong-field trajectory. Those fluctuations can extend cutoffs, phase-lock harmonics, enable otherwise forbidden emission, or tune material response with much weaker classical fields.

## Trade-offs / Contrasts with

- vs classical HHG control — classical control uses waveform, polarization, two-color fields, or phase matching; quantum-light control uses field statistics and fluctuations as additional knobs.
- vs HHG-generated quantum light — here the input is nonclassical; in HHG quantum-state engineering, a classical driver can generate nonclassical output.

## Open questions

- Can quantum-light-driven HHG be made stable enough for spectroscopy, or is its value mostly in fluctuation-sensitive observables?
- What is the best "quantum dial": photon-number variance, quadrature squeezing, polarization squeezing, or spatial structure?
