# Attosecond pulse generation

> Attosecond pulse generation uses phase-locked high harmonics to synthesize light bursts short enough to probe electron motion on its natural time scale.

## Origin

HHG is the workhorse source for attosecond pulses. In this batch, the new twist is that quantum light may change which harmonics are phase locked and how short the synthesized pulse can become — [[attosecond-pulse-synthesis-squeezed-light-hhg-wang]].

## How it works

An HHG spectrum provides a broad set of high-frequency components. To synthesize a short attosecond pulse, many of those harmonics must have controlled relative phase. Classical HHG often phase-locks only part of the spectrum; squeezed-light-driven HHG is proposed to phase-lock a broader or even full HHG spectrum.

## Trade-offs / Contrasts with

- vs [[optical-frequency-comb]] synthesis — combs provide stable line spacing; attosecond HHG synthesis requires strong-field phase control across XUV harmonics.
- vs classical HHG gating — quantum-light control may add phase-locking and fluctuation knobs but raises stability and measurement challenges.

## Open questions

- Can squeezed-light HHG produce experimentally cleaner attosecond pulses, or does the same quantum fluctuation that helps phase locking complicate reproducibility?
- Can integrated or nanostructured HHG sources ever support attosecond-pulse engineering on-chip?
