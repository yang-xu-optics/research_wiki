# Wide-band photonics

> Wide-band photonics is integrated photonics designed to operate across a broad wavelength span, rather than being optimized only for telecom. In this paper the relevant span includes visible, near-infrared, telecom, and potential mid-infrared operation.

## Origin

The term is used in the Si3N4-on-sapphire paper to frame silicon nitride as a platform for nonlinear optics, quantum photonics, frequency combs, visible photonics, and mid-infrared access — [[crack-free-silicon-nitride-on-sapphire-lu]].

## How it works

The platform requirements are different from narrow telecom-only photonics:

- The waveguide material needs a broad transparency window.
- Propagation loss has to stay low across several wavelength bands.
- Waveguide thickness and width must support dispersion engineering and coupling across bands.
- Substrates and claddings matter because they can limit transparency, stress, coupling, or post-processing.

The Si3N4-on-sapphire paper treats [[silicon-nitride]] as the core wide-band material and sapphire as a substrate that may improve thick-film growth, visible/deep-visible access, vertical optical coupling, and mid-infrared operation — [[crack-free-silicon-nitride-on-sapphire-lu]].

## Trade-offs / Contrasts with

- vs telecom photonics — telecom platforms can optimize tightly around 1310/1550 nm; wide-band platforms must manage dispersion, coupling, and loss over much larger spans.
- vs single-function nonlinear devices — wide-band platforms are useful when one chip must support multiple wavelengths or conversion processes.

## Open questions

- Which applications most need the combination of thick Si3N4, sapphire transparency, and low stress?
- How much of the claimed deep-visible and mid-infrared advantage survives full packaging and cladding choices?
