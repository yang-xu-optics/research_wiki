# Bright squeezed vacuum

> Bright squeezed vacuum (BSV) is a macroscopic nonclassical light state with a broad photon-number distribution and squeezed/correlated fluctuations, intense enough to begin driving strong-field processes such as [[high-harmonic-generation]].

## Origin

In this HHG batch, BSV is the enabling light state that makes quantum-light-driven strong-field physics experimentally plausible. It appears as a direct HHG driver, a weak perturbing "quantum dial," and a way to access intensity fluctuations inaccessible to coherent pulses of the same mean energy — [[high-harmonic-generation-bright-squeezed-vacuum-rasputnyi]], [[quantum-dial-hhg-wang]].

## How it works

BSV is generated by strongly pumped, unseeded parametric amplification. Unlike a coherent pulse, its photon-number distribution is extremely broad. That means a single mean intensity can contain rare high-field events, strong quadrature fluctuations, and correlations that modify tunneling, recollision, cutoff, and emitted photon statistics.

## Trade-offs / Contrasts with

- vs coherent laser pulses — coherent light gives deterministic classical fields with shot-noise fluctuations; BSV gives macroscopic quantum fluctuations that can act as a control knob.
- vs weak squeezed light — BSV is bright enough to matter in nonlinear and strong-field regimes.
- vs thermal noise — BSV fluctuations are quantum-correlated and can carry squeezing, not merely random classical intensity noise.

## Open questions
