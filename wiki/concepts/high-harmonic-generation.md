# High-harmonic generation

> High-harmonic generation (HHG) is a nonperturbative nonlinear process in which intense light drives matter to emit radiation at high integer multiples of the driving frequency, often reaching XUV or soft-X-ray photon energies and attosecond time scales.

## Origin

Conventional HHG grew from strong-field and attosecond physics, where a semiclassical three-step picture treats the laser as a classical field and the electron as quantum matter. The new HHG batch reframes that picture: once the optical field is quantized, HHG becomes a route to [[strong-field-quantum-optics]] and nonclassical light generation — [[quantum-optical-nature-hhg-gorlach]], [[entanglement-measurement-theory-hhg-stammer]].

## How it works

The standard gas-phase picture is ionization, acceleration, and recombination. In solids, related electron-hole dynamics create intraband and interband currents. What changes in this batch is not the existence of the nonlinear upconversion, but the observables:

- emitted harmonic modes can be entangled, squeezed, bunched, antibunched, or non-Poissonian;
- a nonclassical driver such as [[bright-squeezed-vacuum]] can reshape cutoff, spectra, phase locking, symmetry, and trajectories;
- condensed-matter structure, topology, and excitons can imprint quantum-optical signatures on the emitted light.

## Trade-offs / Contrasts with

- vs [[second-harmonic-generation]] — SHG is perturbative chi(2) frequency doubling; HHG is nonperturbative, often broadband, and tied to strong-field electron dynamics.
- vs [[optical-frequency-comb]] — combs provide evenly spaced coherent lines; HHG can produce attosecond bursts and extreme upconversion, but with harder control over quantum state and efficiency.
- vs low-order quantum frequency conversion — HHG reaches spectral regions inaccessible to standard chi(2)/chi(3) conversion, but may strongly entangle light and matter rather than preserving an input quantum state cleanly.

## Open questions

- Can HHG become a practical quantum frequency converter into XUV/soft-X-ray regimes?
- Which signatures are easiest to measure experimentally: squeezing, bunching, antibunching, entanglement, or cutoff fluctuations?
- Can solid-state and nanostructured HHG be integrated with photonic platforms from the rest of the vault?
