# Stochastic field theory for HHG and THz generation

> A stochastic field theory for HHG and THz generation remains worthwhile if framed as a boundary-finding and simulation framework: which strong-field observables can be explained by classical stochastic fields, and which require genuinely nonclassical phase-space structure.

## Origin

The new antibunching HHG paper raises the bar for stochastic theories because antibunching is a temporal-correlation signature without a classical positive-P counterpart — [[photon-antibunching-hhg-stammer]]. But the broader source folder explicitly targets stochastic electrodynamics, phase-space representations, BSV mode structure, HHG with noisy/stochastic optical fields, and THz optical-rectification/plasma-current models.

## How it works

The useful split is:

- **Classical stochastic field theory:** treats the driver as random classical electric-field samples. This can model noisy coherent pulses, thermal-like fluctuations, shot-to-shot BSV-inspired intensity statistics, propagation, phase matching, plasma currents, and detector averaging.
- **Quantum stochastic/phase-space theory:** uses Wigner, positive-P, or related representations to sample quantum fields. This can include squeezing and nonclassical correlations, but may require non-positive distributions, doubled variables, or nonclassical noise terms.

The antibunching result says a purely classical stochastic field cannot be the whole story for all HHG observables. It does not make stochastic theory obsolete; it tells the theory where its classical boundary lies.

## Trade-offs / Contrasts with

- vs full Hilbert-space quantum optics — stochastic/phase-space approaches scale better to multimode propagation, macroscopic targets, and detector models, but can hide nonclassicality if the representation is too classical.
- vs semiclassical HHG — stochastic theory keeps field fluctuations and shot-to-shot observables, not only mean spectra.
- HHG vs THz — HHG probes high-frequency photon statistics and harmonics; THz generation probes low-frequency currents, optical rectification, plasma photocurrents, and macroscopic waveform noise. The same stochastic driver can map very differently into these channels.

## Open questions

- What is the minimal stochastic model that reproduces BSV-driven HHG spectra and cutoff fluctuations but fails on antibunching?
- Can the failure point become a clean nonclassicality witness rather than a weakness?
- Are THz observables more sensitive to driver photon-number/quadrature fluctuations than HHG spectra?
- Can one unified stochastic input-output model predict HHG harmonics, THz waveforms, and their cross-correlations under the same quantum-light drive?
