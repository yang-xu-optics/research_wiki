# Electro-optic modulation

> Electro-optic modulation converts an electrical or optical control signal into an optical phase, amplitude, or interference change. In this vault it is a bridge between communications hardware, programmable optics, and active III-V photonic platforms.

## Origin

The III-V batch includes carrier-index theory — [[carrier-induced-index-iii-v-bennett]], optically activated GaAs interferometers — [[optically-activated-gaas-mzi-cheng-tsai]], high-power AlGaAs MZMs — [[low-voltage-high-power-algaas-mzi-bhasker]], and high-bandwidth GaAs/AlGaAs travelling-wave modulators — [[gaas-algaas-travelling-wave-eo-modulator-spickermann]].

## How it works

An MZI turns phase modulation into intensity modulation by changing the relative optical phase between two arms. The phase can be changed by carrier injection/depletion, photogenerated carriers, or a Pockels/electro-optic response. The device-level figures of merit are voltage-length product, bandwidth, optical loss, linearity, and optical power handling.

## Trade-offs / Contrasts with

- vs [[lithium-niobate]] modulation — LiNbO3 is the benchmark for low-loss high-linearity EO modulation; III-Vs offer tighter integration with lasers and nonlinear devices.
- vs [[photoconductive-gain-programming]] — conventional EO modulators are local circuit elements; photoconductive programming treats optical patterns as a large parallel control plane.
